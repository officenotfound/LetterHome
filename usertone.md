# Usertone

Style rules for all written content on Letterhome: page copy, guides, FAQs, meta descriptions, emails. Applies whenever I'm writing sentences a customer or Google will read.

## Core

Get to the point. No filler openers, no throat-clearing, no "great question" energy. Say the thing.

Don't inflate claims. If a page says something is fast, cheap, or easy, that has to be true and specific ($10 CAD, one business day), not vibes.

Demonstrate value through efficient communication. Before a sentence ships, ask: does this need to be said? Can it be said shorter?

## Grammar & punctuation

No em dashes, ever. Use a period, comma, or restructure the sentence instead.

No en dashes except a spaced one ( - ) when nothing else works.

Hyphens only for compound words (non-resident, mail-in).

Use contractions (isn't, doesn't, can't, you're) instead of formal un-contracted forms. Customers read this in a hurry, not a courtroom.

No contrastive syntax ("This isn't X, it's Y"). Just say Y.

No rhetorical negation ("not optional, it's required"). Just say it's required.

Never describe something by first saying what it isn't. Don't open with "This isn't just a mailing service" - say what it is.

No "just" used to wave away a process that actually deserves explaining. If there's a real step (waiting for a Canadian mailing date, calculating a notice period), name it.

## Tone & style

No hedging. Don't write "might," "perhaps," "somewhat," or "could potentially" when the answer is known. State the price, the timeline, the rule.

No marketing fluff, no forced enthusiasm, no exclamation points doing the work a fact should do.

High information density. Every sentence on a landing page should tell the reader something they didn't already know (a price, a deadline rule, a legal requirement), not restate the page title in longer words.

Specific over vague. "Mailed within one business day" beats "fast turnaround." "$10 CAD" beats "affordable."

Avoid AI-writing tells: no "in today's world," no rule-of-three lists for their own sake, no headers that just restate the next sentence, no em dashes, no "it's not just X, it's Y" framing.

## Structure

Keep the site's existing format: hero, how-it-works steps, FAQ with real questions people actually search, related-guides links at the bottom. Don't invent new section types per page.

Legal/government content (LTB notices, CRA mail, IRCC documents) needs the actual rule stated plainly, with a source link, and a one-line disclaimer that it's not legal advice. No padding around the rule itself.
