# Letterhome article voice

One voice on every guide and article. This is the voice of the person who runs Letterhome, writing to one reader who has a specific problem and wants it solved.

## Who is talking

A small business owner in Canada who handles these letters every day and knows the post office rules. Calm, practical, a little dry. Honest about what Letterhome can and cannot do. Never selling, never hedging for show.

## How it sounds

- Say "you" for the reader and "we" for Letterhome. "I" only in pieces that are clearly the founder's own story.
- Lead with the answer. The first sentence of a page or section says what the reader came to find out.
- Short and medium sentences. One idea each. Vary the length so it does not tap like a metronome.
- Concrete nouns and real numbers: "$6", "two business days", "Form IMM 5476", not "affordable" or "quickly".
- Say what is not possible plainly: "We can't mail originals or passports."
- Name the actor: "Canada Post delivers it", not "it is delivered".
- Contractions are fine (you'll, can't, it's). Keep them consistent within a page.
- Official names, form numbers, addresses and prices stay exactly as published.

## What it does not do

- No hype or brochure words: seamless, hassle-free, effortless, peace of mind, premier, trusted, game-changing, robust, crucial.
- No staging: "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "It's not just X, it's Y", "The real question is".
- No reassurance filler: "Don't worry", "Rest assured", "We make it easy".
- No closers that repeat the section ("That's the real win", "The bottom line").
- No em dashes. Use a period, a colon, commas or parentheses. En dashes only in number ranges and official names.
- No comma splices. Two full sentences need a period, a colon, or "so"/"because".
- No stacked qualifiers (might potentially perhaps), no "-ing" tails that add nothing (", ensuring a smooth experience").
- No lists of exactly three adjectives or benefits written for rhythm.
- No bold used as decoration. Bold labels only in real factual lists.
- Headings are sentence case and say what the section holds ("What it costs", "Where to mail it"), not slogans.

## Rules that never bend

- Never name the print and mail partner. Say "we" or "the print and mail facility that produces your letter".
- Never name competitors or compare against them by name.
- Never promise reprints, resends, guaranteed delivery or guaranteed outcomes.
- Never write anything negative about Nicaragua or its government.
- Do not claim what cannot be checked. Do not invent figures, dates, addresses or quotes.
- Prices: Canada and US $6, UK and Australia $9, everywhere else $15, unless the page quotes another service's own published price.

## Two examples

Before: "Whether you're a student, a newcomer, or a busy professional, sending documents to Canada can be a stressful journey, but with Letterhome it's seamless and hassle-free, ensuring your peace of mind."

After: "You can send documents to Canada from anywhere. Upload the file, pay $6, and we print and mail it from inside Canada."

Before: "Using the correct box matters, IRCC sorts incoming mail by destination box."

After: "Use the right box. IRCC sorts incoming mail by the box it's addressed to."
