What is a Canadian mailing date and when do you need one?

Canada CANADA POST
POSTES CANADA
AUG 07 / 2026

A mailing date is the official ink stamp applied by Canada Post when a letter enters the postal system. It records the date and indicates the letter was mailed inside Canada. For legal and government purposes, the mailing date is treated as the official date the document was sent, not when it arrives.

Most people who need a Canadian mailing date are outside Canada but have a document that legally or administratively must be mailed from inside the country. Common situations:

LTB eviction notices

Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act requires landlords to serve N4, N12, and other notices by mail. The mailing date starts the notice period clock.

CRA tax filings

The Canada Revenue Agency uses the mailing date as the filing date for paper returns. A Canadian mailing date protects you from late-filing penalties.

IRCC immigration documents

Some Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada applications require physical mail. Canadian mailing date confirms the submission date.

Legal correspondence

Court documents, demand letters, and legal notices often need proof of mailing from Canada with a specific date on record.

Provincial tribunal forms

BC's RTB, Ontario's LTB, and other provincial tribunals accept mail service with the mailing date as the service date.

General government correspondence

Any government body that requires documents by a deadline, the mailing date proves you submitted on time.

Common questions

Is a Letterhome mailing date a real Canadian mailing date?

Yes. We physically mail your document from inside Canada using Canada Post lettermail. The envelope carries a genuine Canada Post mailing date, not a stamp, not a digital mark, but the actual ink cancellation applied by Canada Post when your letter enters their system.

Can I use this for an Ontario LTB eviction notice?

Yes. The Ontario Residential Tenancies Act accepts mail service, with the notice deemed served 5 days after mailing. Your Letterhome order confirmation records the mailing date, which is what you need to establish for any LTB application. See the LTB forms guide for details on the 5-day rule and termination date calculations.

Does this work for CRA tax deadlines?

Yes. The CRA uses the mailing date as the filing date for paper returns and other correspondence. If you mail your T1 return via Letterhome on April 30, it is treated as filed on April 30, even if it takes Canada Post several more days to deliver it to the tax centre.
