What is an N12 notice?

The N12, officially the Notice to End your Tenancy Because the Landlord, a Purchaser or a Family Member Requires the Rental Unit, is used when a landlord (or their spouse, child, parent, or caregiver) genuinely needs to move back into the rental unit. It requires at least 60 days' notice and the termination date must fall on the last day of a rental period.

The N12 form is available free from the Landlord and Tenant Board website.

Important: This is general information, not legal advice. The N12 process has specific requirements and consequences, including possible compensation obligations and bad faith protections. Consult a paralegal before serving. If your intent isn't genuine, the LTB can find bad faith and impose penalties.

Calculating your 60-day notice period from abroad

The 60-day period can't start until the notice is deemed served. When serving by mail, Ontario law adds 5 days before the notice is considered received, so you calculate 60 days from the deemed service date, not the mailing date.

Example, monthly tenancy, rent due 1st of month:

Order placed + letter mailed: August 7

Deemed served (+ 5 days): August 12

60 days from August 12: October 11

Must fall on last day of rental period → October 31 ← termination date on your N12

Write October 31 on your N12 before uploading. If the termination date is too early, even by one day, the LTB can void the notice.

Who can the N12 be served for?

The person listed as requiring the unit must be one of the following:

  • The landlord themselves
  • The landlord's spouse
  • A child or parent of the landlord or landlord's spouse
  • A person who provides care services to the landlord, their spouse, child, or parent
  • A purchaser of the property (if listed in the agreement of purchase and sale)

The requirement must be genuine. If the landlord serves an N12 in bad faith and the tenant later establishes this, the LTB can order the landlord to pay the tenant up to 12 months' rent.

The landlord compensation requirement

When you serve an N12, you are required to pay the tenant one month's rent compensation, due on or before the termination date. This applies whether or not the tenant vacates voluntarily. Include this obligation in your planning, it isn't optional.

Common questions

Can I serve an N12 if I'm living outside Canada?

Yes. The RTA doesn't require a landlord to be in Canada to serve a notice. What matters is that the notice is properly completed, properly served (by mail to the unit), and that the reason, personal or family use, is genuine.

What if my tenant refuses to leave after an N12?

If the tenant doesn't vacate by the termination date, you file an L2 Application with the LTB. A hearing is scheduled. You can attend remotely. The LTB will review whether the N12 was properly served and whether the need for the unit is genuine.

Can a purchaser of my property serve an N12?

Yes, but only if the agreement of purchase and sale includes a clause allowing the purchaser to serve an N12 for personal or family use, and the purchaser genuinely intends to occupy the unit.
