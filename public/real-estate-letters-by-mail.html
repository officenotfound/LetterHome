A personal letter gets opened more often than a flyer. Here is how agents use them.

Send a letter

Pick a small area, write one letter to the owners with something useful in it, mail it in batches, and follow up. We print, stamp and mail each letter, so you can send a few at a time without a printer.

How it works

  1. Pick a small area

    Choose a street or a few blocks you know well. A short, local list works better than a large one.

  2. Write one letter that is useful

    Tell them something real: what a nearby home sold for, what is happening in the market, or an offer to answer questions.

  3. Mail in batches

    Send a few each week so you can follow up on each batch.

What makes a letter get read

A name and a first-person voice. One idea per letter. A clear next step, such as a phone number. Plain paper and a normal envelope, so it looks like personal mail. Avoid claims you cannot support and always follow your provincial regulator's rules on advertising.

What we do

We print each letter, put it in an envelope and mail it. For a handful of letters, the ordinary send page is enough. For larger batches, see our bulk mailing page.

Keep records

Our dated confirmation emails record when each letter was mailed, which helps when you track which batch produced a reply.

Common questions

Can I put my own return address on the letter?

Yes. Enter your address as the sender when you order.

Is this addressed mail?

Yes. Each letter has its own recipient address, which is different from unaddressed flyers.

How many can I send at once?

See the bulk mailing page for batches. For a few letters, use the normal send page.
