Students move often, and campus mail works differently from home mail. Check the address first.

Send a letter

Ask the student, or the school's mail services, for the exact address. Residence mail often goes to a central mailroom with the student's name and a mailbox or room number, while off-campus students use their home address. Check before you send.

How it works

  1. Ask the student for the full address

    Get the building, room or mailbox number, and the postal code. For residence, the university's mail services page usually shows the format to use.

  2. Use the format the school wants

    Typical order: the student's full name, student number if asked, residence name and room or mailbox, university name, street address, city, province and postal code.

  3. Write and send

    Type your letter online, add the address, and we print it, put it in an envelope and mail it from Canada.

On campus or off campus

First-year students often live in residence where mail is sorted by a central mailroom. Upper-year students rent apartments, so their mail goes to their home address. If you are unsure, ask which one they check.

When parents live abroad

International students often have family overseas. A parent in another country can write the letter online and have it mailed inside Canada, so it arrives like any other letter from within the country. There are no stamps, trips to a post office or international postage to arrange.

What to say

A letter from home is a nice change from texts. Tell them what is happening at home, ask a specific question, and mention an event you are looking forward to. If it is exam season, keep it short and encouraging.

Common questions

Is a student's residence address the same as their home address?

No. Use the residence or mailroom address the school gives. Ask the student or the university's mail services.

Will the student be told a letter arrived?

Some mailrooms post a notice for packages but not letters. Tell your student to expect something.

Can I send documents too?

You can include pages with your letter. For official documents such as transcripts, see our guide on mailing student documents.
