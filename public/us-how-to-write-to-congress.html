How to write
to Congress

Your two senators and your representative work for you. Here is how to reach them by mail.

Send a letter →

Write to the two senators for your state and the representative for your district. Senators get mail at United States Senate, Washington, DC 20510, and representatives at U.S. House of Representatives, Washington, DC 20515, or at the local office on their website. Put your home address on the letter so they know you are a constituent.

Updated November 2026

Find who represents you

Everyone in a state has two senators and one representative. Find your representative by entering your ZIP code on house.gov, and your senators by choosing your state on senate.gov. Both sites link to each member's own website, which lists their Washington and local office addresses.

Write to your own members. Offices focus on people from their state or district, and the Senate notes that many senators will acknowledge but not answer mail from people outside their state.

The addresses

A senator:

The Honorable Jane Doe
United States Senate
Washington, DC 20510

A representative:

The Honorable John Doe
U.S. House of Representatives
Washington, DC 20515

Each member's website gives their exact room and building, and their offices back home. A local office is a good choice for a letter about a local issue or a problem with a federal agency.

What to put in the letter

  1. Your full name and home address, so the office can see you live in their state or district and can reply. The Senate asks for a return address on all mail.
  2. One issue, named in the first sentence. If it is about a bill, give its number, such as H.R. 1234 or S. 567.
  3. Why it matters to you, in a few sentences of your own.
  4. What you want them to do: vote for or against, co-sponsor, or help with a case.

Start with "Dear Senator Doe:" or "Dear Representative Doe:" and close with "Sincerely,". Keep it to one page and be polite. A short letter in your own words carries more weight than a form letter.

Getting help with a federal agency

Members of Congress have caseworkers who help constituents with federal agencies, such as a delayed passport, a Social Security problem or a veterans' benefits claim. Write to the local office, explain the problem and what you have already tried, and include any case or reference numbers.

If it is urgent

A letter takes time to arrive and be answered. If your issue has a deadline, also call the office or use the contact form on the member's website. The U.S. Capitol switchboard at (202) 224-3121 can connect you to any office.

Common questions

What is the address to write to a senator?

The Honorable (name), United States Senate, Washington, DC 20510. Each senator's website gives the exact office address and their state offices.

What is the address to write to a representative?

The Honorable (name), U.S. House of Representatives, Washington, DC 20515. Each representative's website gives the exact office address and the district offices.

How do I find my representative?

Enter your ZIP code on house.gov. Choose your state on senate.gov to find your two senators.

Do members of Congress read letters?

Their staff handle the mail. Offices focus on letters from their own constituents, and a short, personal letter on one issue is the easiest to act on.

Should I write to my senator's Washington office or local office?

Either works. A local office is often better for a local issue or for help with a federal agency.

How should I start a letter to a senator?

"Dear Senator (last name):". For a representative, "Dear Representative (last name):".
