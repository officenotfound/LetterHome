You can legally serve notices from abroad

Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act says nothing about where a landlord must be located when serving a notice. What matters is how the notice is served — and mail is one of the explicitly permitted methods. You do not need to be in Ontario, or even in Canada, to mail a valid N4, N12, or other LTB notice.

What you do need: the letter must enter the Canadian postal system via Canada Post, addressed to the rental unit. Letterhome handles that part.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules around LTB notices change, and the specific facts of your tenancy matter. Confirm current service requirements at tribunalsontario.ca or speak with a paralegal before filing an application.

Which notices you can serve by mail

N4

Non-payment of rent

14-day notice for monthly tenants. Most common notice for non-resident landlords.

N12

Personal use / family

60-day notice if you or a close family member needs to move back into the unit.

N5

Interfering with others

For damage, noise, or other conduct issues. 20-day window to correct first occurrence.

N8

Persistent late payment

At end of term, after repeated late payment not covered by N4.

N13

Demolition or renovation

120-day notice if the unit requires major work and must be vacant.

L1 / L2

LTB Applications

Filed with the Board after a notice period expires without resolution.

The 5-day rule — get this right before you write the date

When you serve an LTB notice by mail, Ontario law deems it served 5 days after it is mailed — regardless of when Canada Post actually delivers it. You must add those 5 days before calculating your notice period.

Example — N4 for non-payment:

Order placed: August 7

Letter mailed by Letterhome: August 8 (next business day)

Deemed served (+ 5 days): August 13

14-day notice period ends: August 27 ← earliest valid termination date on your N4

If you write a termination date that is too early, the LTB can dismiss your application and require you to start again. Always calculate from the mailing date, not the order date.

How to serve a notice from outside Canada

  1. Download the correct form from the LTB website

    All LTB forms are free at tribunalsontario.ca/ltb/forms/. Fill in all required fields, but leave the termination date blank until you know your mailing date.

  2. Place your order at Letterhome and note the mailing date

    Go to /send and upload your filled-in form. We mail next business day. Your order confirmation includes the exact mailing date.

  3. Calculate and confirm your termination date

    Add 5 days to the mailing date (deemed service) then add your required notice period. Make sure the termination date on your form matches.

  4. Keep the confirmation email as your proof of service

    When you later file an L1 or other application, you will need to declare how the notice was served. Your Letterhome confirmation with the mailing date is your record.

What about a return address?

You can use your property's address, a property manager's address, or omit the return address entirely — Letterhome supports all three options. Many non-resident landlords omit the return address since they don't want mail forwarded to a foreign address, and it is not legally required on an LTB notice.

Common questions

Does my tenant need to know I'm outside Canada?

The Ontario RTA does not require you to disclose your location to your tenant when serving a notice. You serve the notice according to the rules; your physical location is not part of the form.

Can my tenant challenge the notice because I'm outside Canada?

Not on that basis alone. The validity of an LTB notice depends on whether it was properly completed and properly served — not on where the landlord lives. A tenant can challenge service if they can show the notice was not actually mailed or was served incorrectly, which is why keeping your mailing confirmation is important.

What if the tenant doesn't respond or doesn't leave?

After the notice period expires, you file the appropriate LTB application (typically L1 for non-payment, L2 for other reasons). The LTB schedules a hearing. You can participate by phone or video from outside Canada — hearings are conducted remotely by the LTB. You will need your proof of service from the original mailing.

Do I need a Canadian address on the notice?

The notice itself must include the rental unit address (where you're sending it). The landlord address section can be your foreign address or a property manager's address in Canada. Check the specific form instructions on the LTB website.
