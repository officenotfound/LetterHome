Keeping in touch
across countries.

A simple habit for staying close to family in another country, one letter at a time.

Why mail still works

Messages are easy and forgettable. A letter is something a person decides to write and someone decides to keep. Families spread across countries often find that a regular letter does more to keep them close than a flood of texts.

A habit that lasts

  1. Pick one person

    Start with a parent, grandparent or sibling. One reliable letter beats a plan for ten.

  2. Pick a rhythm

    Monthly is easy to keep up. Quarterly works too.

  3. Fix a day

    The first Sunday of the month, say. A set day is the difference between a habit and a good intention.

  4. Write a page

    One page, with one photo. It is enough.

  5. Plan for delivery time

    International mail can take up to 4 weeks, so a letter written for a birthday needs to go early.

Ideas for what to put in

  • Something that happened this month, good or bad.
  • A photo of a place they have never seen.
  • A small detail of daily life, like what you cook or what the commute is like.
  • A question for the person writing back.
  • A drawing or a note from a child.
