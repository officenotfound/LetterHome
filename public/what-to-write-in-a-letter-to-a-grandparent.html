a grandparent.

Prompts that always work, and a few tips for making a letter they will keep.

Write about an ordinary day, ask them one question, and add one photo. A page is plenty. If they live in a care home, check the address format first, and we can print and mail it from Canada from $6 CAD.

Start with something small

You do not need news. A grandparent who gets a letter wants to hear your voice, and the ordinary details of your day are what they enjoy most. Pick one of these and write a page.

Prompts that always work

  • Describe today. What you ate, what the weather was, what made you laugh.
  • Share one thing you are looking forward to. A trip, a class, a recipe you plan to try.
  • Ask about them. What was their first job? What was school like? Where did they meet their best friend?
  • Remember something together. A holiday, a meal, a song. Tell them why it stayed with you.
  • Send a photo. One good photo with a sentence about it. Colour printing is $1 extra.
  • Include a question they can answer. It gives them a reason to write back.

Make it easy to read

  • Use short paragraphs and plain words.
  • Keep it to one or two pages so it is not tiring.
  • Mention names and places. Familiar names are the best part.
  • Avoid hard news in a letter that arrives by itself. Call or ask a family member first.

Common questions

How long should a letter to a grandparent be?

One or two pages. Shorter is easier to read and just as welcome.
