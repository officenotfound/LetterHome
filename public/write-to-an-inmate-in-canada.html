Letter writing

How to write to
an inmate in Canada.

Federal institutions accept letters by mail. Put the person's name, the institution, and your return address.

Send a Letter →

For a federal institution, write the inmate's name in the centre of the envelope, then "C/O" and the institution's name, then its address. Put your return address in the top left corner. If you do not know where the person is held, Correctional Service Canada will try to forward a letter sent to a regional headquarters.

How it works

  1. Find the institution

    Use the institution's address from Correctional Service Canada's institution profiles. Provincial jails have their own addresses and rules.

  2. Address the envelope

    The inmate's name, then C/O and the institution's name, then the institution's address.

  3. Add your return address

    In the top left corner, so the person can reply and undeliverable mail can come back.

Location is private: Correctional Service Canada does not disclose where an inmate is held without their consent. If you do not know, send the letter to a regional headquarters and staff will try to forward it.

Correctional Service Canada accepts physical mail, by Canada Post or by hand. All mail goes through the same screening and inspection, and there is normally no limit on how much mail an inmate can receive.

Provincial jails

People serving sentences under two years, or awaiting trial, are held in provincial or territorial facilities. Each province sets its own mail rules and addresses, so check the province's corrections website before you write.

Common questions

How do I address a letter to a federal inmate in Canada?

The inmate's name, then C/O and the institution's name, then the institution's address, with your return address in the top left corner.

Can I find out where an inmate is held?

Correctional Service Canada does not disclose locations without the inmate's consent. Send the letter to a regional headquarters and staff will try to forward it.

Is mail to inmates checked?

Yes. All mail to federal institutions goes through screening and inspection.

Is there a limit on letters?

Normally not, though a warden can set a limit if the volume is disruptive.
