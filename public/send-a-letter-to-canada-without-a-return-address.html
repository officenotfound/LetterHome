Send a letter to Canada without a return address

You can legally send a letter to Canada without a return address. Canada Post and most postal services do not require a return address. Simply leave the return address field blank when ordering through Letterhome.

Updated June 2026

A return address is not required on Canadian mail. Canada Post asks that you include one because it lets them return undeliverable letters to you — but it's a recommendation, not a rule. You are free to leave the return address blank on any envelope, for any reason.

With Letterhome, the return address field is optional. You can send a letter to any Canadian address from anywhere in the world without including your own address on the envelope. The letter is printed and posted from inside Canada, carrying a Canadian postmark.

Why people skip the return address

There are plenty of straightforward reasons to leave it off:

  • Personal safety. People fleeing domestic violence, harassment, or stalking often need to mail something — legal documents, letters to family, correspondence with support organizations — without revealing where they are now. Leaving the return address blank is a basic and legitimate protective measure.
  • Surprise letters and gifts. Part of the fun is that the recipient doesn't know who sent it until they open it. A card, a letter, an announcement — sometimes the reveal belongs inside the envelope, not on it.
  • No fixed address. Travellers, people between moves, expats in temporary housing, or anyone without a stable mailing address may not have a return address to give in the first place.
  • Privacy preference. You simply don't want the recipient — or anyone who handles the envelope — to have your address. That's a reasonable choice.
  • Mailing from abroad. If you're outside Canada and using a service that posts from inside Canada on your behalf, a foreign return address would be confusing or could cause the letter to be routed back internationally if undeliverable. Leaving it blank is often the cleaner option.

One thing to know: if the letter can't be delivered and there's no return address, Canada Post will discard it rather than return it. So it's worth double-checking the recipient's full address — name, street, city, province, and postal code — before you send.
