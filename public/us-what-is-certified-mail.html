What Certified Mail is

Certified Mail is an extra service you add to a First-Class Mail or Priority Mail item. It gives you a mailing receipt and a record of what happened at delivery. USPS describes it as a way to prove you sent something and to see when it was delivered, or that a delivery attempt was made. It is not part of the price of any mail class, so you pay for it on top of postage.

What it costs

The Certified Mail fee is $5.55, plus the postage for the letter itself. The extras are optional.

Does Certified Mail need a signature?

On its own, Certified Mail does not give you a signature record, according to USPS. You get a delivery record: the date and time of delivery, or that a delivery attempt was made. If you need to know who accepted the letter, add Return Receipt, which gives you a record showing the recipient's signature. The recipient may be asked for photo ID before the item is handed over. Restricted Delivery limits delivery to the addressee or someone they authorized, and it has to be bought together with another service such as Certified Mail.

How to send a Certified Mail letter

  1. Prepare the letterAddress it and put the postage on, or have the counter staff do it.

  2. Take it to a Post OfficeBuy Certified Mail there. USPS says to hand the item to a postal employee if you want a postmarked sender's receipt.

  3. Keep the receiptIt is your proof of when you mailed the letter. Store it with a copy of what you sent.

  4. Track itThe label has a tracking number. Enter it on the USPS tracking page to see delivery or an attempt.

How long does Certified Mail take?

It travels at the speed of the mail class you attach it to. On a First-Class letter that is the same time as any other First-Class letter, and our delivery times guide covers what to expect. Certified Mail adds tracking and a record, not speed.

What it does not do

  • It does not prove what was inside the envelope. Keep a copy of the letter and note the date you mailed it.
  • It does not insure the contents. Insurance is a separate service.
  • It cannot be used with every kind of mail. It is available with First-Class Mail and Priority Mail.
  • It does not make a notice legally valid. Some deadlines, leases and legal notices say how a letter must be delivered, so check the rule that applies to you or ask a professional.

When a regular letter is enough

For a card, a letter to a friend or a routine bill payment, a stamp is enough. Certified Mail makes sense when you need to show later that you sent something, such as a dispute, a formal request or a document with a deadline.
