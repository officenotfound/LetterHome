A standard letter up to one ounce needs one Forever stamp (82 cents). That is about four sheets of printer paper in an envelope. Each extra ounce adds 29 cents, up to 3.5 ounces.

Updated November 2026

The short answer

One Forever stamp pays for a letter up to 1 ounce, which is roughly four sheets of plain printer paper in a regular envelope. Past that, each extra ounce costs 29 cents more, and a letter can weigh up to 3.5 ounces before it is priced as a large envelope.

A sheet counts once whether it is printed on one side or both. Metered or online-printed postage is 4 cents lower than a stamp.

How to pay the extra

A Forever stamp always covers the first ounce. For a heavier letter you add postage for each extra ounce, or put a second Forever stamp on it and overpay slightly. The simplest fix is to ask at the post office counter or use the postage calculator to see the exact amount.

When the envelope matters more than the weight

A letter has to be between 3.5 by 5 inches and 6.125 by 11.5 inches and no more than a quarter inch thick. A stiff, square or lumpy envelope costs extra even when it weighs one ounce. If it is bulky or has a card or keepsake inside, ask at the counter.

Skip the stamps

If you would rather not count sheets, type the letter on our site. A letter within the US is $3.00 with postage, envelope and printing included, and extra pages cost a little more. You do not need to weigh anything.

Common questions

How many stamps do I need for a letter?

One Forever stamp for a letter up to 1 ounce, about four sheets of paper in an envelope. Add 29 cents for each extra ounce.

How many sheets of paper can I mail with one stamp?

About four sheets of 20 lb paper in a plain #10 envelope stay under one ounce. Five sheets go over and need extra postage.

How heavy can a regular letter be?

Up to 3.5 ounces. Anything heavier is priced as a large envelope.

Does printing on both sides change the weight?

No. A sheet weighs the same whether it is printed on one side or both.
