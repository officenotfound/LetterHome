A mailed demand letter puts an overdue invoice on the record and is usually a precondition small claims court expects. Here's what to include and how to send it.

Send a Demand Letter →

A demand letter states what's owed, the deadline to pay it, and what happens next if it isn't. Sending it by mail, not just email, is what makes it look, and function, as a formal step rather than another reminder.

How it works

  1. State the facts plainly

    Invoice number, amount owed, original due date, and any prior reminders sent. No need for aggressive language, specificity does the work.

  2. Set a firm deadline

    10 to 14 days is standard. State clearly what happens if the deadline passes, typically referral to collections or small claims court.

  3. Mail it, don't just email it

    A physical letter signals seriousness in a way email doesn't, and gives you a mailing date on record if this ends up in court.

  4. Upload it at Letterhome

    Go to /send, enter the debtor's mailing address, and attach your signed letter.

This isn't legal advice. Requirements for demand letters before small claims filing vary by province. Check your province's small claims court process before relying on this as your only pre-filing step.

Common questions

Do I need a lawyer to send a demand letter?

No, you can write and send one yourself. A lawyer's letterhead can add weight in some cases, but a clear, specific letter from you is often enough.

Is a demand letter required before small claims court?

Many provinces expect or require you to have attempted to resolve the debt directly first, and a mailed demand letter documents that attempt. Check your specific province's rules.

What if they still don't pay?

Your next steps are typically small claims court or a collections agency, depending on the amount and your preference. Keep your demand letter and mailing confirmation as part of your file either way.
