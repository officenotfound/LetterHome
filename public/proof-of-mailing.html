You don't need a lawyer's file system. A copy of what you sent, a confirmation with the mailing date, and knowing where that confirmation lives is enough proof for almost every situation where it comes up later.

How it works

  1. Keep a copy of the letter itself

    Whatever you upload to be mailed, save it. If a dispute ever comes down to what the letter actually said, having the exact file beats trying to reconstruct it from memory.

  2. Get a confirmation with the mailing date

    Every Letterhome order sends a confirmation email showing the date your letter was mailed, the recipient, and the order number. That date is your evidence of when it left your hands, not when it arrives.

  3. Keep the confirmation somewhere you'll find it

    A dedicated email folder or a single starred message works. The order stays in your account under order history too, so it isn't only in your inbox.

Not the same as registered mail: a mailing confirmation proves you sent something on a date. It doesn't prove what was received or that it arrived. If you need that level of proof, see registered mail instead.

Why the mailing date is what matters

For CRA filings, LTB notices, and most deadline-driven mail, what counts legally is the date something was mailed, not the date it's read. That's exactly what a mailing confirmation documents.

Common questions

Does a Letterhome confirmation email count as legal proof?

It documents the date you placed the order and the recipient address, which is the same kind of record a Canada Post receipt provides. Whether it's sufficient for your specific situation depends on the body you're dealing with, when in doubt use registered mail for a signature.

What if I need proof of delivery, not just proof of mailing?

That needs registered mail or Xpresspost with tracking. A standard mailing confirmation only proves the mailing date, not arrival.

Can I get a copy of an old order confirmation?

Yes, log into your account and check order history, or email support@letterhome.ca with your order number.
