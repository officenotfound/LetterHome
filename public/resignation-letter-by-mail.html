How it works

  1. Check your notice obligations

    Your employment contract may specify a notice period. Provincial employment standards set minimums that can apply if your contract doesn't.

  2. Write a short, neutral letter

    Your position, your last working day, and a simple statement of resignation. No need to explain your reasons in writing unless you want to.

  3. Mail a copy for your own record

    Handing a copy to your manager is normal, mailing one as well creates a dated, independent record that you gave notice.

Common questions

How much notice do I actually owe?

Check your employment contract first, then your province's employment standards minimums if the contract doesn't specify. There's no single national rule.

Do I need to explain why I'm resigning?

No. A resignation letter can simply state the fact and your last day, an explanation is optional and not required.

Why mail a copy if I already handed one in?

It creates a dated, independent record outside your workplace's own files, useful if a dispute ever arises about when notice was given.
