BC landlords can serve notices by mail from anywhere

British Columbia's Residential Tenancy Act allows landlords to serve tenancy notices by ordinary mail to the rental unit address. Your location as the landlord, inside or outside Canada, doesn't affect the validity of a properly served notice. What you need is for the letter to be mailed from inside Canada via Canada Post.

All BC RTB forms are available free from the BC government's RTB forms page.

Important: BC tenancy law and RTB forms change periodically. This page is general information. Confirm current form versions and service requirements at the BC Residential Tenancy Branch website or speak with a licensed property manager before filing.

The 5-day deemed service rule in BC

Like Ontario, BC adds 5 days when a notice is served by ordinary mail. The notice is legally deemed received 5 days after the mailing date. Your notice period starts on the deemed service date, not the mailing date.

Example. One Month Notice (RTB-33), monthly tenancy:

Letter mailed: August 7

Deemed served (+ 5 days): August 12

One full rental month from August 12 → must end on last day of a rental month

Effective date: September 30 ← write this on your RTB-33

If the deemed service date is past the 1st of the month, you typically need to go to the last day of the following month to give a full rental period's notice. Check the current BC RTB guidance for your specific situation.

Common questions

Does BC require registered mail for eviction notices?

No. BC's Residential Tenancy Act allows service by ordinary mail (not registered or certified). Regular Canada Post lettermail is sufficient, and the 5-day deemed service rule applies. You don't need tracking or delivery confirmation for the notice to be valid.

Can I email the notice to my BC tenant?

Only if your tenant explicitly agreed in writing to receive notices by email. Without that written agreement, email isn't a valid method of service under BC's Residential Tenancy Act. Mail is the safest method if you don't have that consent documented.

What happens after I serve the notice?

If the tenant disputes the notice, they can apply to the RTB within the period set out on the notice form. If they don't dispute it and don't leave, you file a dispute resolution application with the RTB. Hearings are conducted by telephone or video, you don't need to be in BC or Canada to participate.
