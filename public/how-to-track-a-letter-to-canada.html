How to track a letter sent to Canada

Standard letters to Canada cannot be tracked. Tracking is only available if you used a registered, priority, or courier service. To track, go to canadapost.ca/track and enter your tracking number. If you don't have a tracking number, your letter cannot be traced.

How to track a Canada Post delivery

  1. Go to canadapost.ca/track
  2. Enter your tracking number (starts with letters, e.g. JJ123456789CA)
  3. View the current status and delivery history

Canada Post also offers email and text delivery notifications if you sign up for a free account.

What if my letter to Canada never arrived?

For untracked standard letters, your options are limited:

  • File a missing mail inquiry with the sending postal service (usually online)
  • Contact Canada Post's customer service if the letter was sent within Canada: 1-866-607-6301
  • Standard lettermail is not insured — no compensation is available if it's lost

For tracked mail, your postal service or courier can open a trace or investigation and may offer compensation.

For important documents — use tracked mail or Letterhome

If you're mailing immigration documents, legal paperwork, or anything time-sensitive to Canada, always use a tracked service. The extra cost is worth it.

Frequently asked questions

Can you track a standard letter sent to Canada?

No. Standard lettermail — both domestic Canada Post and most standard international letters — does not include tracking. You need registered, priority, or courier service for tracking.

How do I track a letter sent to Canada from the US?

USPS First-Class Mail International has no tracking. Use USPS Priority Mail International (US$35–60) or a courier for a tracking number. Enter it at tools.usps.com.

How do I track Canada Post delivery?

Enter your tracking number at canadapost.ca/track. Only Xpresspost, Priority, and parcels include tracking — standard Lettermail does not.

What do I do if a letter sent to Canada never arrived?

For untracked letters, file a missing mail inquiry with the sender's postal service. Standard lettermail is not insured. For future important mailings, use a tracked service or Letterhome to avoid international mail risk altogether.
