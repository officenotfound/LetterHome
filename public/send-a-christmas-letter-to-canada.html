Who a mailed letter works best for

Grandparents and older relatives who do not live online, family in retirement or nursing homes, and anyone who will keep a real letter in a drawer long after a message is gone. It is also a good fit if you are a Canadian living abroad and want the letter to arrive with a Canadian postmark, without arranging for someone at home to mail it.

Common questions

When do I need to order for a letter to arrive by Christmas?

As a safe estimate for 2026: order by November 20 for international destinations and by December 5 within Canada. These dates come from typical Canada Post lettermail times (up to 4 weeks internationally, up to 2 weeks within Canada) plus a buffer for holiday volume. They are estimates, not a guarantee.

Can I write it now and have it mailed closer to the holidays?

Yes. Choose Schedule for later at checkout (free) and pick the date you want it mailed. We mail on your chosen date, and normal Canada Post delivery time applies after that.

How much does it cost to send a holiday letter to Canada?

$5 CAD to any Canadian address, $11 CAD to the United States, or $20 CAD to anywhere else, including printing, envelope and postage. Colour printing is $1 extra, which is worth it if you are including photos.

Can I send a card or a gift?

No. We print PDFs and typed letters on letter-size paper and mail them in an envelope. If you have a holiday page you designed, you can attach it as a PDF, but we do not send physical cards, gifts or parcels.

Can I send letters to several relatives?

Each recipient is a separate order. If you are sending to many people, email sales@letterhome.ca and we will help.

Will it arrive by a specific date?

We mail within one business day, or on the date you schedule, but we cannot control Canada Post delivery speed, which is slower in December. Order early. Registered mail adds tracking and proof of delivery, not speed.
