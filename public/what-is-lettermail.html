What is lettermail?

Lettermail is Canada Post's standard service for sending letters and documents within Canada. In 2026, a domestic stamp costs $1.24 (with booklet) or $1.44 individually. Items must weigh under 500g and fit within standard envelope dimensions.

Updated September 2026

Lettermail is Canada Post's standard service for mailing letters and documents. It's the everyday option, buy a stamp, seal an envelope, drop it in a red mailbox. No tracking, no frills, and for most purposes, no need for anything more. It covers personal letters, bills, government forms, and the vast majority of routine correspondence mailed in Canada.

In 2026, a single domestic lettermail stamp costs $1.44, or $1.24 each when bought in a booklet or coil.

Size and weight limits

To qualify for the standard lettermail stamp rate, an envelope must meet these dimensions:

DimensionMinimumMaximum
Length100 mm245 mm
Width148 mm156 mm
ThicknessN/A5 mm
WeightN/A500g (lettermail class max)

Standard envelopes (business size, #10) fall comfortably within these limits. A standard letter-size sheet folded in thirds fits fine.

Items that are too thick, too heavy, or oddly shaped may be classified as a large envelope or parcel and cost more to mail.

How long does lettermail take?

Canada Post doesn't guarantee delivery times for standard lettermail. In practice, delivery within Canada typically takes 2 to 9 business days. The main variables are distance and whether the destination is urban or rural. Major cities tend to be on the faster end; remote or northern addresses can take longer.

A safe planning estimate is up to 2 weeks for anything in Canada. If your letter needs to arrive by a specific date, send it early or consider Xpresspost or Registered Mail for a faster, tracked option.

Is lettermail tracked?

No. Standard lettermail isn't tracked. There are no scans at pickup or delivery, and no way to confirm receipt online. Once it's in the system, you wait.

If tracking or proof of delivery matters, for legal documents, government submissions with deadlines, or anything where you need confirmation. Canada Post offers Registered Mail (with a delivery record) or Xpresspost (faster, with tracking).

What lettermail is used for

Lettermail handles the majority of paper correspondence in Canada: personal letters, greeting cards, bills and invoices, cheques, government notices, tax documents, application forms, and any other flat document that fits in a standard envelope. If it's paper and it fits, lettermail is usually the right service.
