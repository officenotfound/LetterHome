You're generally entitled to ask a collection agency to verify the debt in writing before you're required to respond further. Request that in writing, keep a copy, and don't confirm the debt is yours until you've seen the verification.

How it works

  1. Don't confirm or pay before verifying

    Paying, or even confirming details verbally, can restart a debt's statute of limitations clock in some provinces. Get verification first.

  2. Request written verification of the debt

    Ask for the original creditor's name, the amount, and the basis for the debt. Collection agencies are generally required to provide this on request.

  3. Send your request in writing, by mail

    A mailed letter creates a dated record, stronger than a phone call, that you formally requested verification.

  4. Upload it at Letterhome

    Go to /send, enter the collection agency's address, and attach your signed letter.

This isn't legal advice. Rules around debt collection and limitation periods vary by province. If the amount is significant or you're being threatened or harassed, consider speaking with a paralegal or lawyer.

Common questions

Do I have to pay right away?

No. You're entitled to request verification of the debt first, and shouldn't feel pressured into immediate payment before confirming it's valid and accurately calculated.

What if the debt isn't mine?

State that clearly in writing and request they cease contact or provide documentation proving otherwise. Keep a copy of your letter.

Can a collection agency contact me at any hour?

No, most provinces regulate when and how often collection agencies can contact you. If you're being harassed, that's worth documenting separately.
