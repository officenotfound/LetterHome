The N9 is the tenant's own Notice to End a Tenancy. For a monthly tenancy you owe 60 days' notice, ending on the last day of a rental period. It has to be in writing and delivered to your landlord, mail is an accepted method.

How it works

  1. Download and fill out Form N9

    Get it free from the Landlord and Tenant Board. Enter the termination date, at least 60 days out and matching the end of a rental period.

  2. Upload it at Letterhome

    Go to /send, enter your landlord's mailing address as the recipient, and attach your completed N9 PDF.

  3. We mail it the next business day

    Your N9 goes out via Canada Post with a valid Canadian mailing date. Add a few days for delivery when you calculate your 60-day window.

This isn't legal advice. Rules on termination dates and required notice periods can be specific to your tenancy type. Confirm the current requirements at tribunalsontario.ca before filing.

Getting the termination date right

The N9's termination date has to fall on the last day of a rental period, the day before rent is next due, and be at least 60 days after your landlord receives the notice. Mailing adds delivery time on top of that, so mail early rather than exactly at 60 days.

Common questions

How much notice do I owe on a monthly tenancy?

60 days, ending on the last day of a rental period. Different rules apply to fixed-term leases and other tenancy types, check the LTB's guidance for your situation.

Can I serve the N9 by mail?

Yes. Mail is an accepted method of delivering an N9 to your landlord, but factor in delivery time when calculating your termination date.

Do I need my landlord's exact mailing address?

Yes, use the address you normally send rent payments or notices to, or the address on your lease.
