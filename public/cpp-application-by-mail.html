You can start CPP as early as age 60 at a reduced monthly amount, or as late as 70 for a larger one. There's no single right age, it depends on your own finances and health expectations. The application itself is the same either way.

How it works

  1. Decide when you want payments to start

    You can apply up to 12 months before you want your first payment. Starting earlier reduces the monthly amount, starting later increases it, up to age 70.

  2. Fill out Form ISP1000

    Download the current form from canada.ca. Include your Social Insurance Number and banking details for direct deposit.

  3. Upload it at Letterhome

    Go to /send, enter the Service Canada mailing address for your region, and attach your completed form.

  4. We mail it the next business day

    Your application goes out via Canada Post with a valid Canadian mailing date and a confirmation email as your record.

Common questions

What's the earliest I can start CPP?

Age 60, at a permanently reduced monthly amount compared to starting at 65.

Is it better to wait until 70?

Waiting increases your monthly amount permanently, up to age 70. Whether that's the better choice depends on your own retirement plans, this isn't financial advice, consider speaking with an advisor for your specific situation.

Can I apply from outside Canada?

Yes, CPP is available to Canadians living abroad who qualify. The application is mailed to a Canadian Service Canada address regardless of where you're living.

How much does it cost to mail?

$10 CAD for a domestic Canadian address, covering printing, postage, and mailing.
