The short answer: yes, you can do this from abroad

Every major Canadian province allows landlords to serve eviction and termination notices by mail — and none of them require the landlord to be physically present in Canada. What the law requires is that the notice be mailed inside Canada, via Canada Post, to the rental unit address.

That's the only step you can't do from your couch in Dubai or New York. Letterhome handles it: you upload the notice, we print it and mail it from inside Canada the next business day.

This is general information, not legal advice. Provincial tenancy laws differ and are updated regularly. For Ontario specifically, always confirm current requirements with the Landlord and Tenant Board or a licensed paralegal before filing an application.

Ontario specifically: the 5-day deemed service rule

In Ontario, serving a notice by mail means the notice is legally considered received 5 days after the date it enters Canada Post — regardless of actual delivery time. This means you must add those 5 days before starting your notice period countdown.

For an N4 (non-payment): mail August 7 → deemed served August 12 → 14-day period ends August 26 → that's your termination date. Get this wrong and the LTB can dismiss your application.

The three ways to serve a Canadian eviction notice from abroad

  1. Mail it yourself — from your country

    This does not work. A letter mailed from outside Canada arrives with a foreign mailing date. Canadian provincial tribunals require service via Canada Post, and a foreign mailing date is not accepted as valid Canadian mail service.

  2. Hire a property manager or paralegal in Canada

    This works, but costs significantly more. A paralegal handling a single notice mailing typically charges $100–$300+ including service fees. Property managers charge a monthly retainer. For occasional notices, this is usually overkill.

Common questions

Can a tenant challenge a notice served from outside Canada?

A tenant can challenge improper service — but not solely on the basis of where the landlord lives. If the notice was properly completed and mailed from inside Canada via Canada Post, the landlord's location is legally irrelevant. Keep your Letterhome order confirmation as documentation of the mailing date.

What if I need to attend the hearing?

Ontario LTB hearings and BC RTB hearings are conducted by teleconference or video. You do not need to be in Canada to attend. Most provincial tribunals accommodated remote participation for landlords before it was common practice, and it remains standard.

Can I email the notice instead?

Only if your tenant previously agreed in writing to receive notices by email. Without written consent on file, email is not a valid method of service under most provincial tenancy acts. Mail is the safest option.
