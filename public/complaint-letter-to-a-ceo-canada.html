Letter writing

How to write a complaint
letter to a CEO.

A short, factual letter to head office often gets a stuck complaint to someone who can fix it.

Send a Letter →

Send a one-page letter to the CEO at the company's head office. Give your account or order number, what went wrong, what you have tried, and exactly what you want done by a date. Many large companies pass these letters to a team that handles escalated complaints.

How it works

  1. Find the head office

    On the company's website under Contact, About or Investors. The CEO's name is usually on the leadership page.

  2. Write one factual page

    Your reference number, what happened with dates, what you tried, and what you want by when.

  3. Mail it and keep a copy

    Note the date you sent it, and keep copies of everything.

Stay polite: an angry letter is easier to set aside. Facts, dates and a clear request get results.

A template you can adapt

Dear Ms. Lee,

I am writing about order 48213, placed on September 3, 2026. The laptop arrived damaged on September 8. I reported it the same day (case 77120) and was promised a replacement twice. Nothing has arrived.

I would like a full refund of $1,149.00 by December 10, 2026. Copies of my receipt and emails are enclosed.

Sincerely,
Alex Morgan

If nothing happens

Keep a copy and the date sent. Banks have a federal complaint process through the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, telecom and TV complaints go to the Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services, and your province's consumer protection office handles most other purchases.

Common questions

Can I write directly to a CEO in Canada?

Yes. Address it to the CEO by name at the head office.

How do I find a company's head office address?

On its website under Contact, About or Investors.

What should I include?

Your reference number, what happened with dates, what you tried, and what you want done by a date.

What if the company ignores my letter?

Contact the regulator or complaints body for that industry, or your province's consumer protection office.
