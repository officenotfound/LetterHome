A First-Class Forever stamp costs 82 cents. The price went up from 78 cents on July 12, 2026. One stamp covers a standard letter up to 1 ounce.

Updated November 2026

The current price

A Forever stamp costs 82 cents. USPS raised it from 78 cents on July 12, 2026, as part of an average increase of about 4.8% across its mailing prices. The Postal Regulatory Commission approved the change in May.

One Forever stamp pays for a standard First-Class letter weighing up to 1 ounce. That is about four or five sheets of ordinary paper in a regular envelope. Heavier letters and large or stiff envelopes need extra postage, so weigh them at the counter if you're not sure.

How the price has moved

Stamps have gone up most years recently. The Forever stamp was 73 cents before July 2025, 78 cents from July 2025, and 82 cents from July 2026. USPS has said it wants another increase in January 2027 instead of waiting until July, but that needs approval first.

Old Forever stamps still work

A Forever stamp has no price printed on it. Whatever you paid, it is worth the current one-ounce First-Class price on the day you use it. A sheet bought at 73 cents still mails a letter today. Stamps with a printed value are different: they're worth what's printed, and you add stamps to make up the difference.

When a stamp isn't the cheapest option

For one letter, a stamp is the cheapest way to mail it if you already have a printer, an envelope and stamps at home. If you don't, the costs add up quickly. We print the letter, put it in an envelope and mail it for $3.00 within the US, with postage included.
