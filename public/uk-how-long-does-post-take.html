Updated November 2026

The short answer

First Class post aims to arrive the next working day, Monday to Saturday. Second Class post aims to arrive within three working days. These are targets set by Ofcom, the regulator, and Royal Mail doesn't always meet them.

What changed for Second Class

In 2025 Ofcom allowed Royal Mail to stop Second Class letter deliveries on Saturdays and to deliver them on alternate weekdays instead. Royal Mail started with pilots in a few dozen delivery offices from July 2025, agreed the national plan with the Communication Workers Union in April 2026, and has been rolling it out through 2026.

The three-working-day target for Second Class stays the same. First Class keeps its six-day, next-day service.

How often the targets are met

From 1 April 2026 Ofcom set the targets at 90% of First Class post arriving the next working day and 95% of Second Class within three working days. Both are lower than before. There is also a backstop: 99% of post should arrive no more than two days late.

Royal Mail has struggled to meet the old targets. In 2024/25 it delivered 77% of First Class mail and 92.5% of Second Class mail on time, and Ofcom fined it £21 million.

When the date matters

An ordinary stamped letter can't be tracked. If a letter must arrive by a date, Special Delivery Guaranteed promises next-day delivery by 1pm and comes with tracking. Signed For gives you a signature on delivery but no guaranteed date. Both are sold at Post Office counters.

Common questions

How long does a First Class letter take?

Royal Mail aims to deliver First Class letters the next working day, Monday to Saturday. Ofcom's target is 90% on time.

How long does a Second Class letter take?

Royal Mail aims to deliver Second Class letters within three working days. They are moving to delivery on alternate weekdays, with no Saturday delivery.

Can I track a letter with a normal stamp?

No. Signed For adds a signature on delivery, and Special Delivery Guaranteed adds tracking and a next-day 1pm guarantee.
