The layout

Write the address in the middle or the lower right of the envelope, starting each line at the left. The stamp goes in the top right corner. A return address is optional and goes on the back of the envelope, across the top of the flap, or in the top left of the front.

The address, line by line

Ms A Patel
10 Downing Street
LONDON
SW1A 2AA

The first line is the person's name. The next line is the house number or name and the street. If there is a flat or a building, put it on the line above the street or before the number. Then the post town in capitals, and the postcode in capitals on a line of its own.

What to leave out

  • Commas and full stops. The Post Office advises leaving punctuation out.
  • The county. It is not needed if the post town and postcode are there.
  • Underlining of the postcode or town.

Writing the postcode

A UK postcode has two parts separated by one space: an outward code of two to four characters, such as SW1A, and an inward code of one digit and two letters, such as 2AA. Write it in capitals with no punctuation. It is the most important line on the envelope, because the sorting machines read it first. Letters to someone at a business

Put the person's name first, then the company, then the street, town and postcode. If you have a department, put it on the line after the company name.

Common questions

Where does the postcode go on a UK address?

On its own line, last, in capitals with one space between the two halves and no punctuation.

Do I need to write the county?

No. The post town and postcode are enough.

Should I use commas in an address?

No. The Post Office advises leaving out commas and full stops and starting each line at the left.

Where does the return address go?

On the back of the envelope across the top of the flap, or in the top left of the front. It is optional.
