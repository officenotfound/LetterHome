Getting a letter from Santa
in Canada.

Two ways to do it: the free Canada Post program, or a personalized letter that arrives as a surprise.

Children can write to Santa Claus, North Pole, H0H 0H0, Canada and get a free reply through Canada Post. For a surprise letter instead, we mail one from $12.

Option 1: Canada Post's free Santa program

Children can write to Santa and get a reply, free of charge. Canada Post runs the program and explains how it works on its Santa page. The basics:

  1. The child writes a letter

    Handwritten or typed, to Santa Claus.

  2. Address it

    Santa Claus, North Pole, H0H 0H0, Canada. The postal code is H, zero, H, zero, H, zero, with zeros rather than the letter O.

  3. Add a return address

    Without one, Santa has nowhere to reply to.

  4. Mail it in time

    Within Canada you do not need a stamp. From another country, you pay normal postage from that country to Canada. Canada Post sets a deadline each year, so check its Santa page for this season's date before you count on a reply.

Tips so it lands well

  • Use details that only family would know, like a nickname or a pet. Children notice.
  • Mention one real thing they did this year. It is what they remember.
  • Mail early. December is Canada Post's busiest month, and lettermail has no tracking.
  • Decide who opens the mail. A grandparent abroad can send a letter to a grandchild in Canada without anyone at home arranging it.
